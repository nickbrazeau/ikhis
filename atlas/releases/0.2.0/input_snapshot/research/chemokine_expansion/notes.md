[Heath et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9005985/) now has relevant PDF Methods and Results. Migration used fresh normal-donor eosinophils, a 1.5-hour transendothelial assay and optimal chemokine doses described as usually 12.5 nM. Calcium assays used 10 nM CCL11 or CCL13; antibody preceded chemokine by 40 seconds. Figures 3–4 are distinct from IL5-primed Figure 5. Cross-panel donor overlap is unavailable. The PDF calcium C5a-control prose appears inconsistent with the general specificity interpretation; no separate calcium-control assertion is imported. Numeric antibody concentrations are withheld because text extraction confuses micrograms and milligrams; a PDF screenshot attempt failed.

[Godiska et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2196293/) directly measures human DC migration with chemically synthesized mature MDC. DCs were cultured 6–8 days with 50 ng/mL GM-CSF and 20 ng/mL IL13. The study also detects CCL22 in human monocyte-derived macrophage supernatants by Western blot. These separate source and recipient experiments do not establish a complete source-to-target route. CHO recombinant-ligand production is distinct from the human macrophage secretion assay.

[Katou et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1891894/) supports DC CCL22 protein localization, not extracellular release or causal recruitment. The Methods/figure-legend microscopy subset-count disagreement remains explicit.

[Viney et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3965571/) now supports separate Hut78 migration and primary cultured human Th2 calcium assertions. Figure 3 reports calcium EC50 values of 1.6 nM for CCL17 and 2.5 nM for CCL22; these are assay potencies. Calcium Methods cite a chronic-activation preparation separately from the four-week polarization procedure used elsewhere. Transfectant receptor-blockade and mutant experiments are not assigned to primary human Th2 cells. Murine L1.2 hosts remain distinct from human ligand/receptor identity.

[Agostini et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1891930/) closes the primary-human CXCL10 gap. Rejection-associated human BAL T cells migrated toward 200 ng/mL CXCL10 in a two-hour Boyden assay; 20 μg/mL anti-CXCR3 markedly inhibited migration. The source reports 56 procedures and then 58 biopsy samples; that discrepancy is preserved. Figure 3 shows representative patients, not a cohort-wide effect estimate. Normal/no-rejection migration was untestable because of low cell recovery. Macrophage protein staining is recorded separately. Supernatant neutralization was incomplete (46–79%) in a 300-19 cell-line assay; this is not imported as a primary-human recipient or exclusive CXCL10 route.

[Taub's activation comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7682337/) remains alongside the [later purification comparison](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002217599500087Q). The latter prevents treating failure of an unstimulated preparation as a universal resting-T-cell property. These six assertions still need full-method review. The three [Provost CCL26 assertions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23532518/) now have full Methods and Results support. Ordinary nulls are inconclusive for absence.
