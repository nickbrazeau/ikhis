## Candidate mechanisms and explicit limits

1. **CD28/AP-1 and chromatin opening (MA-M01).** In primary naïve human CD4 cells, CD28 omission reduced nuclear FOS/JUNB. A-FOS electroporation before activation reduced AP-1-bound chromatin opening and an activation-inducible RNA program. The A-FOS experiment supports a narrow AP-1 requirement during CD3/CD28 activation. It does not quantify a natural indirect effect of CD28, rescue CD28 omission, establish individual FOS specificity, or prove a chromatin-to-RNA-to-secreted-protein chain. The accessibility and RNA outcomes are recorded as separate branches. NFAT1 binding persisted at many sites without opening, with IL2 an exception; this is a contextual insufficiency observation, not proof NFAT is dispensable.

2. **STAT5 paralogs and FOXP3 (MA-M02).** STAT5B siRNA reduced FOXP3 RNA and intracellular protein; STAT5A knockdown did not detectably change FOXP3 RNA under the tested unstimulated conditions. No equivalence claim follows from that null. The patient series associates FOXP3-positive Treg fraction with measured suppression but does not test FOXP3 mediation. Healthy knockdown cells and disease-cohort suppression experiments are distinct. Earlier patient reports and siblings are flagged; source-level independence is not presumed.

3. **IL12/TBX21 dependence of an intracellular IFNG response (MA-M03).** In expanded human Tregs, IL12 increased intracellular IFNG, while targeted TBX21 perturbation shifted the response toward the IFNG-low fraction. The pooled screen and separate arrayed follow-up support a narrow regulator-dependence candidate. Neither secreted IFNG nor an IL12-to-TBX21 induction edge is assumed. Editing, cytokine culture, and terminal PMA/ionomycin/brefeldin stimulation are explicit.

4. **FOXP3-controlled RNA and protein programs (MA-M04).** FOXP3 knockout altered an IL12-context transcriptional program and increased intracellular IL2/IFNG responses. The transcriptome and flow experiments used different preparation schedules and donor-pair overlap is unavailable. They are a shared-perturbation profile, not a translation mechanism. The study's prose sometimes says “secreted,” but the pertinent methods use intracellular staining with secretion blockade; extraction retains the measured intracellular protein layer. HIVEP2-specific donor dependence, limited donor numbers, and external ChIP-dataset reuse remain limitations. The human-cell xenogeneic mouse experiments were screened but not converted into human in vivo functional assertions.

5. **IL2RA-dependent signaling and suppression (MA-M05).** In expanded, edited, CD25-negative sorted human Tregs, IL2RA loss reduced IL2-stimulated pSTAT5 and suppression of allogeneic responder proliferation. These are directly measured branches under one receptor perturbation. The stronger claim that pSTAT5 mediates the suppression defect is blocked: no STAT5 rescue or intermediate perturbation was used in the suppression assay. FOXP3 abundance did not detectably change despite reduced suppression, providing a practical counterexample to treating FOXP3 protein as a functional endpoint. Effects on IL2 consumption, persistence, or other receptor outputs remain possible.

6. **IL2-dependent FOXP3 in different cell states (MA-M06).** The 2006 primary study measures FOXP3 RNA at 6 hours and protein at 18 hours in CD25-bright cells, with a weaker/no-detected response in freshly sorted CD25-negative cells. This is a state contrast and a shared-IL2 response profile. Its STAT-motif reporter experiment was conducted in 293 cells, so it cannot by itself complete a primary-human-Treg STAT5-to-FOXP3 transcription mechanism. Clinical IL2 recipient data reuse earlier cohorts and are not used as new independent mechanistic experiments here.

7. **CD3-associated FOXP3 induction and IL2/CD25 blockade (MA-M07).** Anti-IL2 and anti-CD25 inhibited the FOXP3 protein response to soluble anti-CD3 in whole PBMC culture. Excess IL2 competed with lower-dose blockade but failed against the high combined-antibody condition. This supports context-specific involvement of endogenous IL2 activity and CD25. It does not identify the producing cell, prove secretion from intracellular expression, establish direct STAT5 mediation, or establish stable Treg identity. Restimulated FOXP3-negative cells still accumulated IL2 despite FOXP3-positive neighbors at the reported mixture ratio. This cannot be generalized into absence of Treg suppression; no purified suppressor titration or FOXP3 perturbation establishes that broad conclusion.

8. **RBPJ/HDAC3 follow-up (MA-M08).** Public figures describe RBPJ mutant/overexpression, HDAC3 deletion, chromatin measurements, FOXP3 readouts, and direct suppression assays. Main wet-lab Methods and Results remain inaccessible, so the candidate is blocked. The July 2025 author correction adds a competing-interest disclosure; it states no alteration to biological results. The saved publisher version includes the disclosure.
