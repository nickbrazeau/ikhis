# Human immune genetic and regulatory mechanism candidates

Extraction only, under contract 0.3.0. Independent and human review remain pending. The project includes all cytokines and broader immune regulation. This is a bounded, explicitly logged shortlist, not an exhaustive review.

## Supported candidates and limits

- **MG-M01, HeLa DDX39B rescue:** Western blot verifies DDX39B depletion. siRNA-resistant wild-type DDX39B restores endogenous IL7R exon6 inclusion while helicase-defective D199A does not. Direct intermediate manipulation and downstream splicing support a narrow mediation-test candidate. This is not direct biochemical RNA-binding proof or primary-cell transport.
- **MG-M02, activated primary CD4 branch:** The same six-donor protocol yields increased exon6 skipping and altered supernatant IL7R. sh3 raises supernatant signal in all six donors; sh5 does so in only two. Shared-epitope ELISA cannot resolve splice-derived soluble protein. The serial RNA→specific protein transition remains blocked.
- **MG-M03, monocyte contrast:** In 161 reused Fairfax samples, DDX39B RNA at 2/24 h does not correlate with 24-h soluble IL7R; the DDX39B-by-IL7R genetic interaction is not detected. DDX39A RNA is inversely correlated with soluble IL7R. Distinct paralogues and observational tests remain separate; they do not refute the different CD4 protein-depletion experiment.
- **MG-M04, allele translation hypothesis:** A-allele DDX39B UTR reporters reduce normalized Renilla activity/RNA at 24 h in HeLa, with RNA from independent wells. Native DDX39B protein is lower by genotype in separate LCLs. Reporter protein is luciferase, so native translation across systems remains blocked.
- **MG-M05, primary CD4 promoter repression:** CD81 TSS CRISPRi lowers CD81 RNA and flow-measured surface CD81, providing broad primary-cell regulatory evidence. RNA is measured at day 11 and flow at days 4, 6, 8, 11. Shared intervention and reported correlation support a branch; RNA-specific rescue, translation/trafficking mediation and measured RNA-first ordering are absent.
- **MG-M06, historical splice contrast:** Gregory 2007 tests minigenes and 94-control PBMC junction RNA, not protein in those experiments. Human HeLa is separated from rat DT3/AT3. Stable-transfection timing is unavailable; 48-h transient timing belongs only to the separate mutation arm.

## Broader regulation and nulls

Alda-Catalinas 2024 maps a PHF19-upstream enhancer perturbation to TRAF1 RNA in primary CD4 cells at an RA-associated region. K562 mapping is cited by that paper but not independently re-extracted. Exact enhancer coordinates, guide-level effects and donor count require recovery; protein and disease causality are not inferred. The source reports inflated nominal P values under non-targeting controls and uses multiple-guide evidence tiers. KCNN4 enhancer deletion lowers RNA in both validation donors, but not significantly; no channel function or protein is inferred.

Stankey 2024 ETS2 source access was subsequently resolved using complete PMC HTML recovered by the parent through approved curl. Primary Results and Methods now support four narrow assertions: chr21q22 enhancer deletion reduces ETS2 RNA induction (n=4 PrimeFlow panel) and phagocytosis (n=7), while ETS2 coding-exon disruption reduces day6 supernatant IL6 protein (n=8) and phagocytosis (n=7). Two shared-intervention profiles keep these endpoints as branches. All cells derive from healthy-donor CD14-positive monocytes, edited before3 days GM-CSF50 ng/mL then3 days GM-CSF plusTNF50 ng/mL, PGE2/Pam3CSK4 each1 microgram/mL. Panel donor overlap is unavailable. Enhancer deletion leaves BRWD1/PSMG1 RNA unchanged; ETS2 editing leaves viability/lineage markers unaffected in separate panels, IL10 is less affected, and endogenous TNF is not assayed because TNF is added. Native ETS2 protein mediation, exact risk-allele replacement, rescue and remaining experiments still require extraction. Earlier search-only snapshots and access failures remain retrieval history.
